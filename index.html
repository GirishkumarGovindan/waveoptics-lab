1. Principle of Superposition & Coherence (NCERT Sec 10.4)

  • Coherent Sources: Two sources are coherent if they emit light waves of the same frequency (or wavelength λ) and maintain a constant phase difference φ over time:
    y1 = a cos(ωt)    and    y2 = a cos(ωt + φ) y = y1 + y2 = 2a cos(φ2) cos(ωt + φ2)
  • Resultant Amplitude & Intensity: Since intensity is proportional to the square of amplitude (I ∝ R2):
    R = 2a cos(φ2)   ⇒   I = 4I0 cos2(φ2)
  • Incoherent Sources: Two independent light sources cannot produce sustained interference because φ fluctuates randomly every ∼10−10 s. The time-averaged value of cos2(φ/2) is 1/2, yielding uniform illumination Iavg = 2I0.

2. Conditions for Constructive & Destructive Interference

  • Phase Difference & Path Difference Relation:
    φ = 2πλ Δx
  • Constructive Interference (Bright Fringes / Maxima): Occurs when cos(φ/2) = ±1, giving maximum intensity Imax = 4I0:
    φ = 2nπ   ⇒   Δx = nλ    (n = 0, 1, 2, 3, …)
  • Destructive Interference (Dark Fringes / Minima): Occurs when cos(φ/2) = 0, giving minimum intensity Imin = 0:
    φ = (2n + 1)π   ⇒   Δx = (n + 12)λ    (n = 0, 1, 2, 3, …)

Sustained Interference Key Feature: All bright fringes in YDSE have equal peak intensity (Imax = 4I0) and equal fringe width (β).
Fringe Width  β = yn+1 − yn = λDd

1. Position of Bright & Dark Fringes (NCERT Sec 10.5)

  • For D ≫ d, the path difference between waves from S1 and S2 reaching point P at distance y from central maximum O is:
    Δx = S2P − S1P ≈ ydD
  • Position of nth Bright Fringe (Constructive):
    yn = nλDd    (n = 0, ±1, ±2, …)
  • Position of nth Dark Fringe (Destructive):
    y'n = (n + 12)λDd    (n = 0, ±1, ±2, …)

2. Conditions for Sustained Interference

  • The two sources S1 and S2 must be coherent (derived from a single parent source by division of wavefront).
  • The light must be monochromatic (single wavelength λ).
  • Both interfering waves should have equal amplitudes (a1 = a2 = a) so that dark fringes are completely dark (Imin = 0).
  • Angular Fringe Width:
    θ0 = βD = λd   (Independent of screen distance D)

1. Single-Slit Diffraction Conditions (NCERT Sec 10.6)

  • Secondary wavelets from different parts of a single slit of width a superpose on the screen.
  • Condition for Minima (Dark Fringes): Dividing the slit into 2n equal zones cancels wavelets in pairs:
    a sin θ ≈ aθ = nλ   ⇒   θn = nλa    (n = ±1, ±2, ±3, …)
  • Condition for Secondary Maxima (Weak Bright Fringes): Dividing the slit into 2n + 1 parts leaves one part uncancelled:
    θn ≈ (n + 12)λa    (n = ±1, ±2, ±3, …)

2. Interference vs. Diffraction (Board Exam Comparison)

  • Origin: Interference is the superposition of waves from two distinct coherent sources (S1, S2); diffraction is the superposition of secondary wavelets from different parts of the same wavefront passing through a single slit of width a.
  • Fringe Width: In YDSE interference, all bright fringes have equal width β = λD/d. In single-slit diffraction, the central maximum width Wc = 2λD/a is twice as wide as any secondary maximum (λD/a).
  • Intensity Profile: In YDSE, all bright fringes have equal peak intensity 4I0. In diffraction, peak intensity drops rapidly from I0 at the center to ≈ I0/22 for the first secondary maximum.
